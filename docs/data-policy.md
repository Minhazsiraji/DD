# Data Policy — Doctor's Diary

> **STATUS: DRAFT.** Not yet in force. Must be finalised, and reviewed by
> someone qualified to advise on Bangladeshi practice, **before the first real
> patient record is entered.** Until then the system is development-only and
> carries fake data.

## What this software is

Doctor's Diary is a private clinical record for one doctor. It is not a clinic
EMR, not a hospital system, and not a patient-facing health record.

## Who controls the data

**The doctor.** Each doctor's patient records belong to that doctor alone. If a
doctor practises at three places, the records travel with the doctor, not with
any of those places.

A clinic or hospital where a doctor practises does **not** get access to that
doctor's patient records by virtue of hosting them.

## What we store

- **Patient identity and contact**: name, age or date of birth, sex, phone,
  address, and an optional emergency contact.
- **Clinical information** the doctor records: allergies, conditions, current
  medicines, alerts, private notes, and — in later phases — consultations,
  prescriptions, investigations and documents.
- **Operational records**: appointments, queue position, payments.
- **An audit trail**: who did what, when, from which location and IP.

## Who can see it

- **The owning doctor** sees their own patients' full history across every place
  they practise.
- **Reception and location administrators** see only what they need at the
  location where they work: scheduling, queue, and patient contact details.
  They do **not** see private notes, and they do not see conditions or
  medications. This is enforced by database policy, not by hiding buttons.
- **Another doctor** sees nothing. If the same person visits two doctors, that
  is two separate records with no link between them. There is no cross-doctor
  search, no merge, and no shared timeline. Sharing a record with another doctor
  requires an explicit, consented and audited action.
- **We** — the people running the service — do not read patient records as part
  of normal operation.

## Where it is stored

On Supabase infrastructure, with row-level security applied at the database so
that a bug in the application cannot by itself expose another doctor's records.
Signature images are held in a private store and served only through links that
expire within minutes.

## Retention and deletion

Clinical records are **not** deleted when a doctor stops using a location — the
history has to survive, and locations are deactivated rather than removed.

## Artificial intelligence

- AI is **off by default** and requires an explicit opt-in.
- AI has **no write access** to clinical tables. It may suggest; only a doctor
  may accept.
- Patient records are never sent wholesale to an AI provider. Only a restricted,
  purpose-built subset is sent, and every disclosure is logged.
- Safety warnings (drug interactions, allergy conflicts) are produced by
  deterministic rules over the doctor's own data, never generated by AI. AI may
  explain a warning; it may never raise one.

## Accuracy and professional responsibility

Doctor's Diary records what the doctor enters. A BMDC registration number shown
in the app is **self-entered and unverified** — it is not a credential check.
Clinical decisions remain the doctor's.

## Current limitations — read before any real use

- The system runs on free development infrastructure that is **not approved for
  real patient data or commercial clinic use**.
- Retention, account closure and patient-correction processes are not finalised.
- There is no signed data-processing agreement in place.
